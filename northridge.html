Glass Work in Northridge — What You Should Know

Northridge is one of our busiest service cities — 10 minutes from our Reseda base, with a dense mix of single-family homes, apartment complexes, and the CSUN campus corridor that collectively generates more glass calls than almost any other part of the San Fernando Valley. The neighborhood's housing stock runs from modest post-war bungalows near the Reseda border to larger ranch homes in the areas north of Nordhoff, with a significant inventory of 1970s–1990s construction in between.

The 1994 Northridge earthquake was one of the most significant glass events in the history of the San Fernando Valley — virtually every building in the neighborhood had glass damage, and the repair and replacement work that followed introduced the first wave of dual-pane windows to a housing stock that was largely still single-pane. That first-generation dual-pane glass installed between 1994 and 2000 is now at or past its insulating glass unit lifespan. If your Northridge home had post-earthquake window replacement, those windows are 25–30 years old — prime candidates for IGU seal failure presenting as cloudy or foggy glass.

Shower enclosures are the most common residential upgrade we do in Northridge. The standard 1970s–80s Northridge bathroom has a 5-foot alcove with framed bypass doors — functional but dated and prone to hardware corrosion in the track. Frameless pivot door replacements are the most popular upgrade: one panel of 3/8" or 1/2" tempered glass on a single hinge, clean hardware, no track to corrode. We custom-cut to your opening — important in older Northridge homes where alcove dimensions vary from the nominal 60 inches.

Commercial glass in Northridge centers around the Reseda Blvd/Nordhoff corridor, the Northridge Fashion Center adjacent businesses, and the university-area properties along Zelzah and Plummer. Break-in board-up and storefront replacement are the most common commercial calls — we respond within an hour for most Northridge addresses.
